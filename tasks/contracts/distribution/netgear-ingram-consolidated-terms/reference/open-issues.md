# Open Issues — NETGEAR / Ingram Micro Distributor Agreement

Issues a reviewing lawyer should resolve before relying on this file set, in rough order of importance.

## 1. Redacted terms cannot be determined from the public filing

The base Agreement and both amendments were filed with a confidential treatment request, and numerous terms are replaced with `[*]` in the text actually available. These cannot be determined from what is public and must be confirmed against an unredacted copy (from the filer, or under the confidentiality procedure the SEC grant describes) before the terms below are relied on:

- **The additional territory added by the first Amendment and re-confirmed by Amendment #2** (both amendments redact the name of the added territory itself — this is the single most important redaction, since it directly affects the scope of the Territory summarized in `consolidated-terms.md`).
- The length of the exclusivity-review periods and related cure mechanics in Section 10(D) (`[*]` for the marketing-plan review period).
- Several pricing and payment mechanics in Sections 4(C)–(E) and 9(A) of the base Agreement: the price-protection lookback and reporting windows, the credit formula on a price decrease, ordinary and initial-order payment due periods, the product-exchange window and value cap, and the post-shipment holding period before a product may be exchanged.
- The liability caps in Section 16(A) (both NETGEAR's and Distributor's total liability caps are redacted figures tied to "the `[*]` to NETGEAR pursuant to the Agreement").
- The discount percentage in Exhibit 1 (Discount Schedule).
- The content of Exhibit 3 (CMD Named Accounts) and Exhibit 4 / 4a (Marketing Plans), which are referenced throughout but not included in the filed text.

## 2. Amendment #2 misdates the agreement it amends

Amendment #2 (entered into July 15, 1998) states that it amends "their Distribution Agreement ('Agreement') dated October 1, 1996." But the base Distributor Agreement's Effective Date is **March 1, 1996** — October 1, 1996 is in fact the effective date of the **first Amendment**, not the base Agreement. This could mean Amendment #2's drafters simply mislabeled the base Agreement's date, or it could signal confusion about which document chain Amendment #2 actually modifies (the base Agreement as it stood before the first Amendment, or the base Agreement as amended). This inconsistency should be raised with the parties or confirmed against their records before treating Amendment #2 as unambiguously amending the base Agreement as already modified by the first Amendment.

## 3. Signature dates for the base Agreement predate some of its exhibit references

The base Agreement is signed by Distributor on October 16, 1996 and by NETGEAR on November 5, 1996, both after the stated Effective Date of March 1, 1996 and after the first Amendment's October 1, 1996 effective date. This is a minor point but worth confirming does not affect which version of the exhibits (e.g., the Routing Guide, dated "083096") was in force at signing.
