# Issues Memo: ODM Supply Agreement

**To:** Agape ATP International Holding Limited ("Customer")
**Re:** ODM Supply Agreement with Organic Preparations Inc.

## 1. Parties and date

The agreement is between **Organic Preparations Inc.** ("the Manufacturer"), of Port Vila, Vanuatu, and **Agape ATP International Holding Limited** ("the Customer"), of Kowloon, Hong Kong. It is dated **15 January 2018**.

## 2. Key commercial and legal terms

- **Term.** Ten (10) years from signing, renewing automatically for successive ten (10) year terms.
- **Termination for convenience.** Either party may end the relationship for any reason other than breach on six (6) months' written notice.
- **Exclusivity.** The Manufacturer appoints the Customer as sole and exclusive agent for the promotion, sale, marketing, distribution and administration of the Schedule A products, and covenants not to sell those products (or anything under the Schedule A product names) to any other party without the Customer's prior written consent.
- **Minimum purchase requirement.** The exclusive appointment is granted for the ten-year term and continues for an indefinite period after that only if the Customer keeps meeting the minimum annual purchase quantities set out in Schedule B. If the Customer falls short, the basis for exclusivity beyond year ten falls away.
- **Products outside Schedule A.** The Customer may source, promote and sell other products outside Schedule A, provided they are non-competing with the Schedule A product range.
- **Right of first refusal.** The Manufacturer must offer the Customer the first right of refusal to buy the intellectual property in the Schedule A products, on agreed terms.
- **Payment.** 50% of the total order cost is due on placing the order; the remaining 50% is due before the goods leave the Manufacturer's warehouse.
- **Inspection and rejection.** The Customer must notify the Manufacturer of any rejection of defective product within seven (7) days of receipt. Product not rejected within that window is deemed accepted.
- **Insurance.** The Customer must maintain product liability insurance throughout the term covering all products bought from the Manufacturer, naming the Manufacturer as additional insured.
- **Cure period.** A rectifiable breach must be cured within 21 days of notice before the non-breaching party can act on it.
- **Governing law / disputes.** The agreement is governed by the laws of England. Disputes that are not resolved by consultation go to arbitration before the Arbitration Committee of the International Chamber of Commerce, located in Switzerland, in English.

## 3. Issues that create risk for the Customer

1. **Change-of-control termination right.** Either party may summarily terminate if control of the other party passes to persons the non-transferring party regards, in its absolute discretion, as unsuitable, or if a transfer of voting stock results in a change in effective control. Because this is framed as the counterparty's unilateral, subjective judgment, the Manufacturer could use a future change in the Customer's ownership (e.g., an investment round or listing) as grounds to end the supply relationship, including the exclusivity that the Customer's business may depend on.
2. **Assignment restriction tied to termination.** Neither party may assign or transfer its rights or obligations under the agreement without the other's prior written consent, and either party assigning or attempting to assign without that consent is itself a ground for summary termination. For the Customer, this limits its flexibility in any restructuring, financing or sale of its business without the Manufacturer's sign-off.
3. **Exclusivity depends on hitting minimum purchase volumes.** The Customer's exclusive distribution rights after year ten are conditional on continuing to meet Schedule B's minimum annual purchase quantities across all eight products. A downturn in any one product line could jeopardize exclusivity for the whole Schedule A range.
4. **One-sided intellectual property position.** The Manufacturer owns all IP in the Schedule A products and in the book *How to Achieve Super Health beyond 2000 - Advanced Edition*, and the Customer's rights are limited to a right of first refusal to purchase that IP on terms still to be agreed, not a present ownership or license interest.
5. **Insurance cost falls on the Customer.** The Customer bears the ongoing cost and administrative burden of maintaining product liability insurance naming the Manufacturer as additional insured, for the life of the agreement.
6. **Dispute forum is remote from both parties.** Neither party is based in Switzerland, so arbitration under the ICC there will add cost and inconvenience to any dispute, and the agreement is governed by English law rather than the law of either party's home jurisdiction.
7. **Short rejection window for defective product.** The Customer has only seven days after receipt to notify the Manufacturer of a rejection before the product is deemed accepted, which is a short window if the Customer's own inspection or distribution chain introduces delay.
